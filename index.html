What a Drug Inspector does

Under Rules 51 and 52 of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945:

  • Inspects licensed drug manufacturing units: the plant and process, how drugs are standardised and tested, storage, and the technical staff's qualifications
  • Takes samples of drugs from manufacturers and dealers for test or analysis
  • Investigates spurious drugs and related complaints
  • Launches prosecutions for breaches of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and its Rules

Who was eligible

  • Bachelor's degree in Pharmacy or Pharmaceutical Sciences, or Medicine with specialisation in Clinical Pharmacology or Microbiology, from a recognised university or institute
  • No experience required and no desirable qualifications listed, so fresh graduates could apply
  • Citizen of India, or a subject of Nepal or Bhutan, or another category UPSC allows with a Government of India eligibility certificate

Recruitment by selection

How UPSC selects

  1. Apply

    Online on upsconline.nic.in. Closed 17 Jul 2026.

  2. Shortlist

    With many applicants, UPSC shortlists, for example by holding a Recruitment Test.

  3. Interview

    Out of 100 marks, with a minimum score for each category.

  4. Appointment

    MoHFW issues the orders. Two years' probation.

Minimum interview marks, out of 100

If UPSC holds a Recruitment Test, you also need its minimum level, which UPSC decides for each recruitment. The test's pattern, marking and length are not in the advertisement; we'll add them here when UPSC announces them.

Other ways UPSC may shortlist

  • Higher educational qualifications than the minimum, such as M.Pharm or a PhD
  • More relevant experience than the minimum, counted before or after the degree
  • A Recruitment Test (RT), held at UPSC's test centres across India

If you're called for the interview, bring the printout of your online application, the original certificates for every claim you made, self-attested copies and a photo ID. Without them, you can't sit the interview.

Pharmakaksha's coverage

What we cover

The advertisement gives no syllabus for a Recruitment Test. This list is our own, built from the post's qualification and duties. We'll match it to UPSC's syllabus if UPSC publishes one.

Drugs and Cosmetics Act and Rules Core
  • Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940: definitions, misbranded, adulterated and spurious drugs and cosmetics, offences and penalties
  • Inspectors: appointment, powers and procedure (Sections 21–23), duties under Rules 51 and 52, sampling
  • Drugs Rules, 1945: licences to manufacture and sell, conditions, labelling and the Schedules (C, C1, F, H, H1, M, X, Y and others)
  • Medical Devices Rules, 2017; New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019
Allied pharmacy laws Law
  • Pharmacy Act, 1948
  • Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985
  • Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954
  • Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013 and NPPA; Pharmacovigilance Programme of India
Pharmaceutics, GMP and quality Pharmaceutics
  • Dosage forms; Physical Pharmaceutics; Pharmaceutical Engineering
  • Industrial Pharmacy; packaging and stability
  • Good Manufacturing Practices (Schedule M), Quality Assurance and validation
  • Biopharmaceutics and Pharmacokinetics
Pharmaceutical chemistry and analysis Chemistry
  • Pharmaceutical Inorganic and Organic Chemistry; Medicinal Chemistry
  • Indian Pharmacopoeia: monographs, limit tests and assays
  • Pharmaceutical Analysis and Instrumental Methods
Pharmacology and pharmacy practice Pharmacology
  • Pharmacology of all systems; chemotherapy; toxicology
  • Adverse drug reactions and drug interactions
  • Pharmacy Practice and Clinical Pharmacy
Microbiology, biologicals and pharmacognosy Allied
  • Pharmaceutical Microbiology: sterilisation, sterility testing, microbial assays
  • Biotechnology; vaccines and other biologicals
  • Pharmacognosy; herbal drugs; Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani drugs under the Act
